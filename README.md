# Proof of Succession

This repository is the evidence index for one account succession: [CY83R-3X71NC710N](https://github.com/CY83R-3X71NC710N) (retired) to [Epsilon0xCell](https://github.com/Epsilon0xCell) (current). It exists because the retired account cannot post, sign in, or vouch for anything, so the succession is documented here instead, in links any reader can check one by one.

## The claim

One builder stands behind both accounts. The old account opened in April 2022 and was built over four years into 163 repositories and a nationally ranked open source record. Work stopped from it in September 2026, and everything continues from the new account, with the project histories migrated in full.

## The strongest evidence: the name in the history

The old account's commit history carries the owner's full legal name as the commit author. The name is deliberately not printed anywhere in this repository: it is listed on the owner's LinkedIn, and GitHub prints it at the top of every commit page linked from this repository.

Counted on 30 October 2026 with GitHub's own commit search, enumerated repository by repository until the collected set matched the search total exactly:

- **2,867 commits** carry the name as author.
- They span **32 repositories**.
- Their author dates run from **26 January 2023 to 20 May 2023**. In that period the builder committed under his legal name. After May 2023 his git identity switched to his handle, which is why the name layer is concentrated in those months.
- The layer predates the successor account (created 21 September 2026) by more than three years, so it cannot have been planted to support this succession.

## Why two accounts comply with the GitHub Terms of Service

GitHub's Terms of Service, Section B.3, state: "One person or legal entity may maintain no more than one free Account." This succession complies with that rule:

- **One account is maintained.** Epsilon0xCell is the only account the owner can sign in to, post from, or change.
- **The old account is inaccessible, not maintained.** Its two factor authentication was lost. GitHub Support declined to restore access, and GitHub's own documentation for that situation states that access is permanently lost and directs the user to create a new account. The successor account, created in September 2026, is that guidance being followed.
- **Nothing is being evaded.** The old account was never suspended or restricted, and the relationship between the two accounts is declared publicly, here and on the profile. A covert second account is the opposite of what this repository is.

## What this repository does not claim

Copied code proves nothing by itself: everything the old account published is public, and anyone can copy public work. The evidence offered here is the name layer in the history, which a copyist cannot borrow without it pointing at someone else, and the unbroken lineage of the projects themselves.
